Charitable Remainder Trusts

A Charitable Remainder Trust (CRT) is a tax-exempt, irrevocable trust that allows individuals to convert highly appreciated assets into a potential income stream while ultimately benefiting a charitable organization. CRTs are popular among philanthropically inclined investors and high-net-worth individuals who seek to reduce income taxes, avoid capital gains tax, receive an income stream, and make a charitable gift.

Structure & Function

When the trust is created, the donor transfers assets—typically appreciated securities, real estate, or other valuable property—into the trust. The CRT sells these assets tax-free and reinvests to generate income.

During the income phase, the trust pays a stream of income to one or more non-charitable beneficiaries (usually the donor or family members) for a specified period—either up to 20 years or for the life of the beneficiaries. At the end of the trust term, the charitable remainder phase begins: the remaining assets are transferred to one or more designated charitable organizations.

  • Charitable Remainder Annuity Trust (CRAT): Pays a fixed dollar amount annually regardless of performance.
  • Charitable Remainder Unitrust (CRUT): Pays a fixed percentage (minimum 5%) of the trust's annually recalculated value.

Tax Benefits

  • Charitable Income Tax Deduction: The donor receives an immediate income tax deduction in the year the trust is funded. The deduction is based on the present value of the remainder interest ultimately passing to charity, calculated using IRS actuarial tables and the Section 7520 rate.
  • Capital Gains Tax Deferral: CRTs can sell appreciated assets without triggering capital gains tax—allowing the full value to be reinvested to generate income.
  • Estate Tax Reduction: By removing assets from the donor's estate, a CRT can help reduce estate taxes. Many donors establish an Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT) to partially or fully replace the charitable value diverted.
  • Income Stream: Tailored income distributions for retirement planning or family support.

Use Cases

  • Donors with low-basis, highly appreciated assets seeking diversification.
  • Individuals wanting to support a charitable cause while maintaining an income stream.
  • Estate planning strategies aiming to reduce taxable estates and provide for heirs.

Limitations & Considerations

  • Irrevocable: Once funded, a CRT cannot be revoked; the donor relinquishes control of assets.
  • Complexity & Costs: Requires legal and tax expertise to structure and administer a CRT properly, which can incur setup and administrative fees.
  • Payout Requirements: IRS rules require that at least 10% of the initial trust value is expected to pass to charity; otherwise, the trust will not qualify.

Charitable Remainder Trusts

A Charitable Remainder Trust (CRT) is a tax-exempt, irrevocable trust that allows individuals to convert highly appreciated assets into a potential income stream while ultimately benefiting a charitable organization. CRTs are popular among philanthropically inclined investors and high-net-worth individuals who seek to reduce income taxes, avoid capital gains tax, receive an income stream, and make a charitable gift.

Key Benefits

  • Immediate charitable income tax deduction
  • Avoid capital gains tax on appreciated assets
  • Potential lifetime income stream
  • Estate tax benefits
  • Professional asset management
  • Support charitable causes

Types of CRTs

  • Charitable Remainder Annuity Trust (CRAT): Pays a fixed dollar amount annually
  • Charitable Remainder Unitrust (CRUT): Pays a fixed percentage of trust assets, revalued annually
  • Net Income with Makeup Charitable Remainder Unitrust (NIMCRUT): Pays the lesser of the trust's net income or the stated percentage
  • Flip Charitable Remainder Unitrust: Begins as a NIMCRUT and converts to a standard CRUT upon a triggering event

Important Considerations

  • Trust is irrevocable
  • Minimum 5% annual payout required
  • Maximum 50% payout allowed
  • Minimum 10% charitable remainder required
  • Complex structure requiring professional guidance
  • Investment restrictions may apply
